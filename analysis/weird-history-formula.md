## The title formula

Almost every title is a **curiosity gap**, not a keyword dump:

1. **"Why..." framing** — "Why The German Army Feared The Night Witches", "Why Elvis Wanted The Beatles OUT Of America". Promises an answer, forces a click to get it.
2. **"The Real..." framing** — "The Real Truth Behind Catch Me If You Can", "The Real Nightmare That Inspired Open Water". Piggybacks on pop culture the viewer already knows.
3. **Contradiction / correction** — "We've Been Wrong About the Trojan Horse", "Why This Princess's Life Was No Fairy Tale". Challenges what the viewer thinks they know.
4. **Superlative + dread** — "The Most Terrifying Summer In New York History", "The Depraved History Behind..."

Notice what's **absent**: no "Top 10", no double pipes `||`, no hashtag-style keyword stuffing, no emoji, no channel name in the title. (HistOddities' older titles do all of these — that's the biggest single change to make.)

## The script formula (consistent across videos)

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:30)** — Drop the viewer into the single most absurd moment of the story, told with deadpan irony. Often ends with a rhetorical question. No "hello and welcome."
2. **Signature CTA (0:30–0:45)** — Short and self-aware: "But before we get into it, be sure to subscribe to the Weird History channel, and leave a comment letting us know what other weird stories you'd like to hear about. OK — [themed transition line]." The transition line is customized per video ("Let's get weird", "Grab a shovel", etc.).
3. **Chronological narrative in chapters (0:45–end)** — The story told start to finish, broken into on-screen chapter cards. Context first ("to understand X, you need to know Y"), then escalation.
4. **The narrator's voice is the product** — Dry, sarcastic, talks TO the viewer ("You'd think... you'd be wrong"), constant modern-day comparisons and pop-culture asides, treats historical figures like coworkers who made bad decisions.
5. **Irony beats and dark humor** — Every gruesome fact gets an understated punchline rather than shock treatment.
6. **Outro (last 30s)** — Wraps the story's aftermath, then asks a direct engagement question ("So, what would YOU have done...?"), then "check out this video next."

## Video specs

- **Length:** 8–14 minutes for standard episodes (compilations 1hr+ come later, stitched from existing episodes — a good long-term library play for HistOddities too).
- **Visuals:** Ken Burns pans over paintings/archival photos, simple animated cutouts with googly-eye-style humor, map graphics, on-screen chapter titles.
- **Thumbnail:** One face or one object, big 3–5 word text that does NOT repeat the title, bright rim lighting on dark background.

## What HistOddities already has going for it

The niche is identical (Emu War, tulip mania, dancing plague, Roanoke, bizarre Roman punishments are ALL Weird History-style topics). The gap is packaging: titles, cold-open hooks, and a consistent narrator persona. The script in `scripts/` applies the full formula.
