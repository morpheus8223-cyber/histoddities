# Full Thumbnail Audit + License-Free Image Sources

This supersedes the earlier `thumbnail-evidence-audit.md` conclusions for the existing catalog. That doc compared my drafts against *other channels'* top thumbnails. This audit looks at **all 19 of HistOddities' own existing thumbnails** for the first time — and the finding changes the recommendation.

## The big finding: the channel already has an established, sophisticated visual brand — and it's good

All 18 pre-Cadaver-Synod thumbnails share a consistent, deliberate design language that's more advanced than either my invented gold-serif style or the plain-sans-serif style I found from History Matters/HistoryDose. This is very likely built with a template/font pack or a paid designer — it uses **layered graphic-design elements a single AI image generation can't produce on its own**: drop-shadow banners, torn-paper dividers, polaroid-style photo collages, circle-and-arrow "look here" callouts, diagonal splits.

**Recurring conventions found across the 18:**
- **Bold, huge, high-contrast display text** — condensed sans-serif or stylized distressed caps, never more than 3–4 words as the dominant element
- **Strict color coding:** white = neutral emphasis, **red = danger/death/violence**, **yellow/gold = title labels or top-10/numbers**
- **Circle-and-arrow "evidence" callout** — appears on at least 3 separate videos (Palme's suspect photo, Cleopatra's bowl of red ochre, the Tulip Bulb price tag) — a proven, repeated in-house device for "look at this specific detail"
- **Versus/split compositions** for conflict stories — Emu War (soldiers vs. emus, diagonal torn-paper divider), Football War (two screaming players + tanks + a soccer ball), Stray Dog War (uniformed soldiers each side, a dog's face in the center)
- **Hyper-specific numbers as the dominant graphic**, not just in the title — Dracula's thumbnail says "HE KILLED 80000 PEOPLE" in huge text; Tulip's says "$100000"
- **Dramatic photoreal or hyperreal close-up faces/characters** with strong emotion (Kaspar Hauser's wide-eyed shock, the Black Death's plague doctor, the Dracula warrior)
- **Illustration style already varies appropriately by topic** — Egyptian gold and pharaoh imagery for Dendera and Cleopatra, medieval illustrated skeletons for the Dancing Plague, gritty photoreal military scenes for the wars — so the "vary style by era/topic" principle was already being followed, just not by me

**The actual outlier: the live Cadaver Synod thumbnail.** It's the only one of the 19 using the "aged oil painting + gold gradient serif" look — a style that doesn't match any of the other 18. It was made without reference to the channel's own established brand, the same gap that caused the Palme redo issue. It isn't *bad* on its own terms, but it currently breaks visual consistency with the rest of the catalog rather than extending it.

## Revised recommendation: extend the existing brand, don't replace it

The planned 50-credit batch (redo top-10 + produce the remaining 8) should **not** proceed with the plain-sans-serif style modeled on History Matters/HistoryDose. Those channels' simpler style was real evidence, but it's the wrong reference class — it's evidence from "quirky fact" explainer channels, not from channels producing dark/mystery/dramatic content, which is most of HistOddities' actual catalog. **The channel's own 18 thumbnails are the better evidence base**, since they're proven-enough (built with real design intent, not auto-generated) and already topic-matched to this exact content mix.

Concretely, going forward, new thumbnails should borrow the established in-house devices: bold condensed caps text with the red/yellow/white color coding, the circle-and-arrow callout where a specific object/detail is the hook, versus-splits for conflict stories, and hyper-specific numbers rendered large when the story has one. Whether to also redo the live Cadaver Synod thumbnail to match this brand (rather than leave it as the current stylistic outlier) is worth deciding before spending any credits on the other 9.

## License-free real image sources (tested, confirmed working)

Yes — real, usable historical images exist for most topics in this catalog. Tested live just now:

- **Wikimedia Commons** (`commons.wikimedia.org`) — best general-purpose source. Confirmed multiple usable Olof Palme photographs with clear licensing (including Swedish institutional archive scans released under open licenses), and confirmed **the actual famous public-domain photo of the 1919 Boston Molasses Flood aftermath** (`File:BostonMolassesDisaster.jpg`) is available and correctly tagged. Every file page states its exact license — always check that tag per-image, never assume.
- **Library of Congress** (`loc.gov/photos`) — confirmed API access works; strong for pre-1929 US photography and news photography, which covers most of the WWI/WWII/early-20th-century American topics in the catalog (Molasses Flood, WWII stories). Has an explicit rights/access field per item.
- **Smithsonian Open Access** (`api.si.edu/openaccess`) — confirmed API access works; everything returned through Open Access is explicitly CC0.
- **Other reliable sources not live-tested here but well-established:** NYPL Digital Collections (flags public-domain items explicitly), Rijksmuseum and the Met's Open Access (both CC0, strong for older European art/portraiture — useful for Cleopatra/Dendera-era imagery drawn from real paintings rather than AI), US National Archives (federal works are PD by default), Europeana (aggregates many European museums/archives with per-item rights flags).

**Practical workflow per video:** search Wikimedia Commons first (fastest, largest, clearest licensing), fall back to LOC/Smithsonian for US-specific historical material, verify the specific license tag on the specific file before using it — "hosted on Wikimedia Commons" is not itself a license, the per-file tag is what matters. Real photos are strongest for topics with actual surviving photography (Palme, the Molasses Flood, WWII stories) or real paintings (Cleopatra, Dendera); topics with no surviving visual record (Cadaver Synod, the Dancing Plague, ancient/medieval events) still need generated or illustrated art, which is what the existing thumbnails already do well for those cases.

## Open decision before any more spend

1. Should the live Cadaver Synod thumbnail be redone to match the established in-house brand (bold caps + red/yellow/white + possibly a circle-callout device), rather than stay as the current stylistic outlier?
2. For topics with real surviving photography (Palme, Molasses Flood, WWII pieces), do we want to source and composite real licensed photos into the existing template style, versus generating new art?
3. The remaining 8 not-yet-made catalog thumbnails and any future videos should target the in-house brand — confirm before producing.
